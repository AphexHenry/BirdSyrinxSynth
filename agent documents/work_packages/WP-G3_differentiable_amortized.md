## 1. Goal

Explore two ways to make fitting faster or better: (1) a **differentiable twin** of the Tier-1 voice in JAX for gradient-based refinement, validated against the C++ engine; (2) **amortised inference**: a neural network trained on synthetic renders that predicts control trajectories directly from a spectrogram, used as an initialiser for E3. Deliver measurements and a recommendation, not a product feature.

## 4. Specification

### 4.1 Differentiable twin

- Same equations and discretisation as C++ (research §4); float64 enabled.
- Parity: 50 ms renders for 10 parameter sets within 1e-6 relative of C++ output before decimation (chaotic/irregular regimes excluded and flagged).
- Gradients: check with finite differences on 5 cases (relative error < 1e-3) in periodic regimes.
- Gradient refinement: windows of 40 ms with state carry (like E3), MRSTFT + E1 feature losses (differentiable versions), gradient clipping, 200–500 steps; compare with CMA-ES (E3) on the benchmark: final loss, wall time, failure cases (near thresholds).
- Final validation of any gradient-found solution is **always** done by rendering with the C++ engine.

### 4.2 Amortised inference

- Training data: ≥ 200 000 synthetic syllables (50–300 ms) generated by sampling voice banks (Variation 0–2) and archetypes, rendered by the C++ engine with random bird constants near the fitted ones, with degradations (noise 15–40 dB SNR, reverb, level changes); targets: E1 control curves (tone, oec, gape, p, δ = 0) at 5 ms resolution + voicing.
- Input: log-mel or linear log-magnitude spectrogram (1 ms hop), plus the D2 f₀ track as an extra channel.
- Model: small 1-D conv + bidirectional GRU or transformer encoder (≤ 10 M parameters), outputs per-frame means and log-variances (Gaussian NLL loss).
- Evaluation on the E1 benchmark and on real syllables: use predictions as θ₀ for E3; compare final loss and runtime vs E2's Viterbi initialisation.

## 5. Suggested steps

2. Gradient checks; gradient refinement on benchmark.
